10×10 Bedroom Layout: Fit the Essentials without Crowding the Room

A compact bedroom can accommodate a double bed, two bedside tables, a wardrobe, a dressing table and a small study desk when each piece is measured first and storage is chosen carefully.

Published: 23 November 2026

This example assumes a clear internal room size of 10 feet by 10 feet (120 × 120 inches, or approximately 3.05 × 3.05 metres). The usable floor area is 100 square feet (about 9.29 square metres). These are inside measurements between finished walls, not the outside dimensions of the building.

The concept uses a 54 × 75 inch double bed with its headboard on the north wall. Two compact bedside tables sit beside it. A sliding-door wardrobe is placed along the south-west side, a dressing table is positioned beneath an east-facing window, and a shallow study desk sits near the south-east corner. The entrance is shown on the south wall.

How to Arrange the Five Main Zones

1. Place the bed first

A 54 × 75 inch double bed is the practical starting point for this compact room. Centre its headboard on the north wall and use two narrow bedside tables. A queen-size bed, often around 60 × 80 inches, may fit physically but will reduce the already limited side and foot clearances. Measure the exact bed frame, including its headboard and any projecting legs.

2. Choose a sliding-door wardrobe

The example places a 48-inch-wide wardrobe along the south-west side. Sliding doors are useful in a small room because they do not swing into the walking area. A 22-inch depth is a compact assumption; check the internal hanging space and the manufacturer's required depth. If full-length hanging storage is essential, a deeper wardrobe may be needed and another furniture item may have to be reduced or removed.

3. Put the dressing table near natural light

The dressing table sits on the east side below the example window, with a wall mirror above it. Check the sill height, curtain movement, privacy and direct sunlight before choosing the final position. Add a suitable mirror light rather than relying only on daylight.

4. Keep the study desk compact

A shallow 30 × 16 inch desk is shown near the south-east corner. It is intended for a laptop or a small monitor, not a large desktop workstation. Use a stool that can slide fully underneath and keep cables secured away from the doorway. If you need a full-size computer desk, consider removing the separate dressing table or using a combined vanity-and-study surface.

5. Use the bedside tables for layered lighting

Two small lamps provide reading light on either side of the bed. A ceiling light can provide general illumination, while a focused desk lamp and mirror light support work and dressing. Have fixed electrical points, switches and wiring planned and installed by a qualified local electrician.

Clearance and Comfort Checks

  • Bed sides: The two bedside tables and double bed use most of the room width. The remaining side gaps are compact, so test the arrangement with tape on the floor and make sure the bed can be made comfortably.
  • Foot of the bed: Keep the central route as open as possible. The desk stool is shown tucked under the desk when not in use; pulling it out will temporarily reduce the walking space.
  • Wardrobe access: Sliding doors avoid a door-swing conflict, but drawers and internal fittings still need usable access.
  • Door movement: Confirm the actual entrance width, hinge side and swing. Do not assume an outward-opening door is permitted or suitable for every property.
  • Windows and ventilation: Keep the window operable and do not block required ventilation, heating or cooling equipment.
Small-bedroom trade-off: Fitting every requested item into a 10×10 room means choosing compact furniture. If you want a queen bed, a deep wardrobe or a full-size computer desk, the most comfortable solution may be to combine the dressing and study functions or move one item to another room.

What to Measure Before Buying Furniture

  1. Measure the clear wall-to-wall dimensions at floor level and note skirting boards, columns and wall projections.
  2. Record the entrance and window widths, their exact positions, sill height and door swing.
  3. Measure the bed frame, wardrobe depth, vanity, desk and stool—not just the mattress size.
  4. Mark sockets, switches, lighting points, air-conditioning units and other fixed features.
  5. Use masking tape to mark each furniture footprint on the floor and test the walking route.
  6. Check that the bed and wardrobe can be carried through the entrance and any hallway or stairway.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a queen bed fit in a 10×10 bedroom?

It may fit, depending on the bed frame and door and window positions, but it leaves less space for bedside tables, storage and movement. This example uses a 54 × 75 inch double bed to make the other furniture more realistic.

Can I fit both a dressing table and a computer desk?

Yes, in this concept they are separate compact pieces. The desk is shallow and best suited to a laptop. A larger computer setup may require a combined vanity-and-study desk or the removal of another item.

Is this a construction-ready floor plan?

No. It is a furniture layout concept based on assumed internal dimensions. It does not show structural walls, electrical circuits, ventilation design, fire-safety provisions or building approvals.

Important Planning Note

Room sizes, accessibility requirements, door rules and building regulations vary by country and property. This example is general design inspiration only. Verify the actual site measurements and have any structural, electrical, ventilation or code-related decisions checked by appropriately qualified local professionals before renovating or building.
