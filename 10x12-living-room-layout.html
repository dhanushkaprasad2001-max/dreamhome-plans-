10×12 Living Room Layout: A Practical Furniture Plan

A compact living room can still feel welcoming when the main furniture, two separate doorways and everyday walking routes are planned together.

Published: 15 November 2026

This guide uses a room with an assumed clear internal size of 12 feet wide by 10 feet deep (approximately 3.66 metres by 3.05 metres). That gives about 120 square feet (11.1 square metres) for seating, television viewing and circulation. The measurements describe the usable room area, not the outside dimensions of the building.

The suggested arrangement places a compact sofa along the north wall, a television unit on the opposite south wall and a coffee table in the centre. It also shows two doorways on opposite side walls: one near the south-west corner and another near the north-east corner. Keeping the openings apart helps the room connect to two different parts of the home without placing both doors beside each other.

How This 10×12 Layout Works

1. Keep the sofa on the longer wall

A sofa placed along the 12-foot north wall uses the longest uninterrupted surface. A model around 6 to 7 feet wide leaves space for a small side table and avoids filling the whole wall. Measure the sofa including arms, not only the seat cushions.

2. Place the television opposite the seating

The south wall gives the television a clear position facing the sofa. Select a low, shallow console and check the screen size and viewing distance against the manufacturer's guidance. Avoid placing a bright window directly behind the screen if glare is a problem.

3. Use a small centre table

A compact coffee table can sit on a 5-by-7-foot rug between the sofa and television. Leave enough room to walk around it and to stand up comfortably. If the room feels crowded, replace the table with two small nesting tables or a movable ottoman.

4. Protect the routes between both doors

The drawing places one doorway on the west wall near the south end and another on the east wall near the north end. This gives the room two distinct connections. Keep a direct, uncluttered route between them; do not put a tall cabinet, plant stand or loose cable in the walking path. As a practical starting point, aim for roughly 30–36 inches (76–91 cm) of clear passage where the room allows, then confirm local accessibility and building requirements.

Windows, Lighting and Electrical Planning

  • Daylight: The example window is centred on the north wall above the sofa. Adjust its position to suit the real exterior view, privacy and sun direction.
  • General lighting: Use a ceiling light or suitable recessed lighting to illuminate the whole room evenly.
  • Reading light: A floor lamp or wall light beside the sofa can provide focused light without using a large table.
  • TV power and data: Plan safe power and data points at the television wall before decorating. Avoid extension leads crossing doorways.
  • Switches: If both doors are used regularly, ask an electrician whether switching arrangements at both entrances would be useful and permitted.

What to Measure Before Buying Furniture

  1. Measure the clear internal width and depth at floor level, including any columns or wall projections.
  2. Record the exact position and width of both doors, their opening direction and the space needed outside the room.
  3. Measure each window, sill height, curtains and any radiator or air-conditioning unit.
  4. Mark sockets, switches, internet points and fixed fittings on a simple sketch.
  5. Use masking tape on the floor to outline the sofa, table and console before purchasing them.
  6. Check that furniture can pass through the entrance, hallways and stairways during delivery.
Small-room tip: In a 10×12 living room, one correctly sized sofa usually works better than a large sectional plus several extra chairs. Choose pieces that leave the door routes visible and easy to use.

Common Questions

Is a 10×12 living room large enough for a sofa and television?

Yes. A compact sofa, a shallow TV console and a small coffee table can fit in a 120-square-foot room when their sizes and positions are checked carefully. The final result depends on door locations, windows and the furniture selected.

Can this layout work with two doors?

It can be a useful starting arrangement when the doors are on separate side walls. The drawing shows them near opposite corners to keep the openings apart. If either door must swing into the room, redraw its swing arc before choosing the sofa, rug or storage.

Can I use an L-shaped sofa?

Possibly, but a large L-shaped sofa may narrow the passage between the two doors. Measure the full footprint and test it with tape on the floor. A compact straight sofa is the safer starting point for this example.

Important Planning Note

This layout is an educational furniture-planning concept. It is not an architectural, structural, electrical or code-compliant construction drawing. Actual dimensions, door widths, accessibility, fire safety, ventilation, electrical work and building approvals depend on the property and local regulations. Ask qualified local professionals to check the design before renovation or construction.
