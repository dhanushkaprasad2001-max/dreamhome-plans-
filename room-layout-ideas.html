Plan the Room Before You Choose the Furniture

A useful room layout is more than a collection of furniture symbols. It should show the room's clear internal dimensions, the position of doors and windows, the size of the main furniture and the walking routes between them. These details help you judge whether an arrangement is practical before you buy furniture or begin a renovation.

Our examples are starting points rather than fixed rules. Room dimensions, building standards and accessibility requirements differ between properties and countries. Measure your own space and check important decisions with an appropriately qualified local professional.

Five Checks Before You Finalise a Room Layout

  1. Measure the usable room. Record the clear internal length and width, then note any alcoves, columns or sloping ceilings.
  2. Mark every opening. Measure doors and windows, note which way each door swings and keep furniture clear of those movement areas.
  3. Place the largest item first. Position the bed, sofa, dining table or main work surface before adding smaller pieces.
  4. Protect the walking routes. Check that people can reach doors, storage and frequently used areas without squeezing between furniture.
  5. Check light and services. Consider daylight, ventilation, sockets, lighting and any plumbing or heating equipment that affects the arrangement.
The layouts on DreamHome Plans are illustrative design concepts, not measured construction drawings. Verify all dimensions on site and have structural, electrical, plumbing and code-related decisions checked by qualified local professionals.
