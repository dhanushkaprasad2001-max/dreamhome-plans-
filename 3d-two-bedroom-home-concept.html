3D Two-Bedroom Home Design Concept

See how a compact single-storey home could be arranged before choosing finishes. This concept combines two bedrooms, an open kitchen and dining area, a practical living room and a simple covered entrance.

Published: 16 November 2026

This illustrative design uses a rectangular footprint of approximately 28 × 32 feet (896 square feet, or about 83.2 square metres). It is intended as an early space-planning example for readers comparing compact home layouts. The covered front porch is additional and is not included in the footprint figure.

The design is organised around a clear central passage. The living room and first bedroom sit near the front, while the kitchen and dining area occupy the rear-left side. A second bedroom and the bathroom are grouped on the rear-right side. Actual wall thicknesses, structure, door swings and site conditions will change the final usable dimensions.

Exterior and Roof Concept

A simple contemporary exterior would suit the rectangular footprint. One option is a warm-white rendered wall finish, a navy or charcoal accent around the entrance, natural timber-look screening and dark-framed windows. Keep the main façade calm and use one or two materials as accents rather than adding too many competing finishes.

  • Roof form: a simple gable roof is one practical concept to investigate. A ridge running along the longer building direction may create a straightforward roof shape, but the final structure and drainage depend on the site and climate.
  • Front entrance: use the covered porch to make the entry easy to identify and to provide shade or rain protection.
  • Windows: place openings to bring daylight into the living room, bedrooms and kitchen while considering privacy, glare, cross-ventilation and local energy rules.
  • Outdoor connection: where the property allows, a rear door or larger kitchen window can connect the dining area to a small garden or patio.

Doors, Windows and Furniture Checks

Before fixing the layout, draw every door swing and check that it does not collide with a bed, wardrobe, kitchen cabinet or another door. Keep a clear route from the entrance through the central passage. Check that bedroom windows can be reached and operated safely, and that furniture does not block them.

Measure the actual sofa, beds, wardrobes, refrigerator, cooker, washing machine and bathroom fittings you intend to use. A room can look spacious in a 3D picture but feel crowded when real furniture and door clearances are added.

Electrical, Lighting and Plumbing Planning

  • Plan general lighting, task lighting and convenient switches at room entrances.
  • Place living-room outlets around the intended seating and media positions, not only where they are easiest to draw.
  • Coordinate kitchen appliance circuits, countertop outlets, ventilation and extractor arrangements with a qualified electrician.
  • Locate bathroom and laundry water supplies, drainage, ventilation and waterproofing details with a qualified plumber or building professional.
  • Review the route between the kitchen and bathroom service areas early; the concept separates them, so a local designer may suggest a mirrored or revised arrangement to simplify plumbing.

Construction Stages to Plan For

  1. Site and brief: confirm the plot dimensions, access, levels, drainage, orientation, setbacks and household needs.
  2. Design development: have the concept converted into dimensioned architectural, structural and services drawings suitable for the local approval process.
  3. Foundation: select the foundation only after site conditions and structural requirements have been assessed.
  4. Walls and openings: set out the walls, door openings and window openings from the approved drawings.
  5. Roof and weather protection: complete the designed roof structure, covering, gutters and rainwater disposal.
  6. Services and finishes: install and test electrical, plumbing and ventilation systems before completing surfaces, fixtures and fittings.

Budget Planning Without Guesswork

A reliable construction budget cannot be calculated from a concept image alone. Costs vary substantially between the USA, UK, Canada, Australia and other markets, and also depend on the site, labour, specification, approvals, utilities and finish level. Ask local contractors for itemised estimates based on the same approved drawings and specification.

Compare the same cost headings in each quote: site preparation, foundations, structure, roof, windows and doors, plumbing, electrical work, insulation, finishes, kitchen and bathroom fittings, external works, professional fees, permits and a contingency allowance. Confirm the currency, tax treatment, exclusions and quote date.

Important: This is a design inspiration concept, not a ready-to-build plan. A qualified local architect, engineer and relevant building professionals must verify dimensions, structure, fire safety, accessibility, energy performance, drainage and planning approvals for the actual property.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 896 square feet enough for a two-bedroom home?

It can be enough for a compact two-bedroom layout, but comfort depends on household needs, storage, circulation, local standards and how the rooms are furnished. Test the arrangement with real furniture dimensions before committing to it.

Can this design be mirrored?

Yes, the room arrangement can be explored as a mirrored concept. A designer should check sunlight, views, access, drainage and the location of existing services before choosing the orientation.

Can this illustration be used for construction?

No. It is an explanatory concept only. Construction requires properly coordinated, dimensioned drawings and professional checks for the specific site and jurisdiction.
